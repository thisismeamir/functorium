# Euclidean Topology

The Euclidean topology on $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the topology generated by open balls.  An open ball in $\mathbb{R}^n$ centered at $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with radius $r > 0$ is defined as:

$$B(x, r) = \{ y \in \mathbb{R}^n : \|y - x\| < r\}$$

where $\| \cdot \|$ denotes the Euclidean norm.

A set $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is open in the Euclidean topology if for every $x \in U$, there exists an open ball $B(x, r)$ such that $B(x, r) \subseteq U$.

Equivalently, a subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is closed if and only if it is the complement of an open set.  Closed sets in the Euclidean topology include:

*   $\mathbb{R}^n$ itself
*   The empty set $\emptyset$
*   Singletons $\{x\}$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$
*   Finite unions of closed balls
*   Arbitrary intersections of closed sets.
