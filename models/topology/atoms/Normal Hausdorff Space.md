# Normal Spaces

## Normal Space 

A normal space is a topological space in which any two disjoint closed sets have disjoint open neighborhoods. Such spaces need not to be Hausdorff in general. A normal Hausdorff space is called a $\mathrm{T}_{4}$ space. 

A topological space $X$ is called a normal space if, given any disjoint closed sets $E,F$ there are neighborhoods $U$ of $E$ and $V$ of $F$ that are also disjoint. More intuitively, this conditions says that $E$ and $F$ can be separated by neighborhoods.

A $\mathrm{T}_{4}$ space is a $\mathrm{T}_{1}$ space that is normal; this is equivalent to $X$ being normal and Hausdorff.

## Completely Normal Space

A completely normal space, or hereditarily normal space, is a topological space $X$ such that every subspace of $X$ is a normal space. It turns out that $X$ is completely normal if and only if every two separated sets can be separated by neighborhoods. Also, $X$ is completely normal if and only if every open subset of $X$ is normal with the subspace topology.

## Perfectly Normal Space

A perfectly normal space is a topological space $X$ in which every two disjoint closed sets $E$ and $F$ can be precisely separated by a function, in the sense that there is a continuous function $f$ from $X$ to $[0,1]$ such that $f^{-1}(\{ 0 \})=E$ and $f^{-1}(\{ 1 \})=F$. This is a stronger separation property than normality.
