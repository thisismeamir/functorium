# Closed Set Topology

Let $X$ be a topological space and $F \subseteq X$. Then $F$ is a closed subset of $X$ if its complement $X \setminus F$ is an open subset.

**Properties:**

*   $X$ and $\emptyset$ are closed subsets of $X$.
*   The union of finitely many closed subsets of $X$ is a closed subset of $X$.  That is, if $F_i$ are closed sets for $i = 1, \dots, n$, then $\bigcup_{i=1}^n F_i$ is also closed.
*   The intersection of arbitrarily many closed subsets of $X$ is a closed subset of $X$. That is, if $F_i$ are closed sets for all $i \in I$ (where $I$ is an arbitrary index set), then $\bigcap_{i \in I} F_i$ is also closed.

A topology on a set $X$ can be defined by describing the collection of closed subsets, with open subsets being those whose complements are closed.
