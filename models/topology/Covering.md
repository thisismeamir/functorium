# Cover

A cover of a set $X$ is a family of subsets of $X$ whose union is all of $X$. More formally if $C=\{ U_{\alpha}:\alpha \in A \}$ is an indexed family of subsets of $U_{\alpha} \subset X$, then $C$ is a cover of $X$ if:
$$
\bigcup_{\alpha \in A}U_{\alpha}=X
$$

other than this definition if $Y$ is a subspace of a topological space $X$ then a covering is a collection of subsets of $X$ that contains $Y$. 

A cover is said to be locally finite if every point of $X$ has a neighborhood that intersects only finitely many sets in the cover. Formally

$$
C= \{ U_{\alpha} \}
$$

is locally finite if, for any $x \in X$, there exists some neighborhood $N(x)$ of $x$ such that the set is finite. A cover of $X$ is said to be point finite if every point of $X$ is contained in only finitely many sets in the cover. A cover is point finite if locally finite, although the converse is not necessarily the case. 

If all of the covering sets are open sets in the topology of $X$, the covering is called open covering.
