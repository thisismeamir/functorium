In [mathematics](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Mathematics "Mathematics"), the **Sierpiński space** is a [finite topological space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Finite_topological_space "Finite topological space") with two points, only one of which is [closed](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Closed_set "Closed set").[[1]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Sierpi%C5%84ski_space#cite_note-1) It is the smallest example of a [topological space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Topological_space "Topological space") which is neither [trivial](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Trivial_topology "Trivial topology") nor [discrete](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Discrete_topology "Discrete topology"). It is named after [Wacław Sierpiński](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Wac%C5%82aw_Sierpi%C5%84ski "Wacław Sierpiński").

The Sierpiński space has important relations to the [theory of computation](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Computability_theory "Computability theory") and semantics,[[2]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Sierpi%C5%84ski_space#cite_note-2)[[3]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Sierpi%C5%84ski_space#cite_note-3) because it is the [classifying space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Classifying_space "Classifying space") for [open sets](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Open_sets "Open sets") in the [Scott topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Scott_topology "Scott topology").

## Definition and fundamental properties

Explicitly, the Sierpiński space is a [topological space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Topological_space "Topological space") _S_ whose underlying [point set](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Set_\(mathematics\) "Set (mathematics)") is {0,1}![{\displaystyle \{0,1\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/28de5781698336d21c9c560fb1cbb3fb406923eb.svg) and whose [open sets](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Open_set "Open set") are {∅,{1},{0,1}}.The [closed sets](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Closed_set "Closed set") are {∅,{0},{0,1}}.So the [singleton set](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Singleton_set "Singleton set") {0}![{\displaystyle \{0\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/0ff0df9ef65c0572eb676580ce1c02b8ec40f694.svg) is closed and the set {1}![{\displaystyle \{1\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/5acdcac635f883f8b4f0a01aa03b16b22f23b124.svg) is open (∅={}![{\displaystyle \varnothing =\{\,\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/4fc7431a30c639b0bf3f504430dce4c51b7736e2.svg) is the [empty set](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Empty_set "Empty set")).

The [closure operator](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Kuratowski_closure_axioms "Kuratowski closure axioms") on _S_ is determined by{0}¯={0},{1}¯={0,1}.

A finite topological space is also uniquely determined by its specialization preorder. For the Sierpiński space this [preorder](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Preorder "Preorder") is actually a [total order](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Total_order "Total order") and given by0≤0,0≤1,1≤1.

## Topological properties

The Sierpiński space S![{\displaystyle S}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/4611d85173cd3b508e67077d4a1252c9c05abca2.svg) is a special case of both the finite [particular point topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Particular_point_topology "Particular point topology") (with particular point 1) and the finite [excluded point topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Excluded_point_topology "Excluded point topology") (with excluded point 0). Therefore, S![{\displaystyle S}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/4611d85173cd3b508e67077d4a1252c9c05abca2.svg) has many properties in common with one or both of these families.

### Separation

- The points 0 and 1 are [topologically distinguishable](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Topologically_distinguishable "Topologically distinguishable") in _S_ since {1}![{\displaystyle \{1\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/5acdcac635f883f8b4f0a01aa03b16b22f23b124.svg) is an open set which contains only one of these points. Therefore, _S_ is a [Kolmogorov (T0) space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Kolmogorov_space "Kolmogorov space").
- However, _S_ is not [T1](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/T1_space "T1 space") since the point 1 is not closed. It follows that _S_ is not [Hausdorff](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Hausdorff_space "Hausdorff space"), or T_n_ for any n≥1.![{\displaystyle n\geq 1.}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/27f9176dbc447117f4bf4eb2cbe6749f669e60ab.svg)
- _S_ is not [regular](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Regular_space "Regular space") (or [completely regular](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Completely_regular "Completely regular")) since the point 1 and the disjoint closed set {0}![{\displaystyle \{0\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/0ff0df9ef65c0572eb676580ce1c02b8ec40f694.svg) cannot be [separated by neighborhoods](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Separated_set "Separated set"). (Also regularity in the presence of T0 would imply Hausdorff.)
- _S_ is [vacuously](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Vacuous_truth "Vacuous truth") [normal](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Normal_space "Normal space") and [completely normal](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Completely_normal_space "Completely normal space") since there are no nonempty [separated sets](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Separated_set "Separated set").
- _S_ is not [perfectly normal](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Perfectly_normal_space "Perfectly normal space") since the disjoint closed sets ∅![{\displaystyle \varnothing }](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/00595c5e33692e724937fdcc8870496acce1ac74.svg) and {0}![{\displaystyle \{0\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/0ff0df9ef65c0572eb676580ce1c02b8ec40f694.svg) cannot be precisely separated by a function. Indeed, {0}![{\displaystyle \{0\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/0ff0df9ef65c0572eb676580ce1c02b8ec40f694.svg) cannot be the [zero set](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Zero_set "Zero set") of any [continuous function](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Continuous_function_\(topology\) "Continuous function (topology)") S→R![{\displaystyle S\to \mathbb {R} }](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/20d6457fd2a3b069c6f5ee2dfa4fae10c7abc915.svg) since every such function is [constant](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Constant_function "Constant function").

### Connectedness

- The Sierpiński space _S_ is both [hyperconnected](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Hyperconnected "Hyperconnected") (since every nonempty open set contains 1) and [ultraconnected](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Ultraconnected "Ultraconnected") (since every nonempty closed set contains 0).
- It follows that _S_ is both [connected](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Connected_space "Connected space") and [path connected](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Path_connected_space "Path connected space").
- A [path](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Path_\(topology\) "Path (topology)") from 0 to 1 in _S_ is given by the function: f(0)=0![{\displaystyle f(0)=0}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/8d308c32c9894b88115262081194321ae7d9bbf3.svg) and f(t)=1![{\displaystyle f(t)=1}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/383b435dd9b14d25c5f0ade17d251bdcbc5361b4.svg) for t>0.![{\displaystyle t>0.}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/785c5fdf1bd18c08fd2c8f4705ba45d0fa5e305b.svg) The function f:I→S![{\displaystyle f:I\to S}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/dd277614957a1cc91bb1a2b8edf705fc207d1244.svg) is continuous since f−1(1)=(0,1]![{\displaystyle f^{-1}(1)=(0,1]}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/79974656147e997f63f80a752d7e45435eb7fd4d.svg) which is open in _I_.
- Like all finite topological spaces, _S_ is [locally path connected](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Locally_path_connected "Locally path connected").
- The Sierpiński space is [contractible](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Contractible_space "Contractible space"), so the [fundamental group](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Fundamental_group "Fundamental group") of _S_ is [trivial](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Trivial_group "Trivial group") (as are all the [higher homotopy groups](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Higher_homotopy_groups "Higher homotopy groups")).

### Compactness

- Like all finite topological spaces, the Sierpiński space is both [compact](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space "Compact space") and [second-countable](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Second-countable "Second-countable").
- The compact subset {1}![{\displaystyle \{1\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/5acdcac635f883f8b4f0a01aa03b16b22f23b124.svg) of _S_ is not closed showing that compact subsets of T0 spaces need not be closed.
- Every [open cover](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Open_cover "Open cover") of _S_ must contain _S_ itself since _S_ is the only open neighborhood of 0. Therefore, every open cover of _S_ has an open [subcover](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Subcover "Subcover") consisting of a single set: {S}.![{\displaystyle \{S\}.}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/930d241ced43dfcaa9262d31956f65ce0a4a3095.svg)
- It follows that _S_ is [fully normal](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Fully_normal_space "Fully normal space").[[4]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Sierpi%C5%84ski_space#cite_note-4)

### Convergence

- Every [sequence](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Sequence "Sequence") in _S_ [converges](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Limit_of_a_sequence "Limit of a sequence") to the point 0. This is because the only neighborhood of 0 is _S_ itself.
- A sequence in _S_ converges to 1 if and only if the sequence contains only finitely many terms equal to 0 (i.e. the sequence is eventually just 1's).
- The point 1 is a cluster point of a sequence in _S_ if and only if the sequence contains infinitely many 1's.
- _Examples_:
    - 1 is not a cluster point of (0,0,0,0,…).![{\displaystyle (0,0,0,0,\ldots ).}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/97b56d383f0c588aa9972cf857f4e35bb7bc6367.svg)
    - 1 is a cluster point (but not a limit) of (0,1,0,1,0,1,…).![{\displaystyle (0,1,0,1,0,1,\ldots ).}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/8601f78e85127dfe62c959500ba93cce5ea9e7f4.svg)
    - The sequence (1,1,1,1,…)![{\displaystyle (1,1,1,1,\ldots )}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/1cc1e200359daf514c55b9ad2ddaf2e15311019c.svg) converges to both 0 and 1.

### Metrizability

- The Sierpiński space _S_ is not [metrizable](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Metrizable_space "Metrizable space") or even [pseudometrizable](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Pseudometrizable_space "Pseudometrizable space") since every pseudometric space is [completely regular](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Tychonoff_space "Tychonoff space") but the Sierpiński space is not even [regular](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Regular_space "Regular space").
- _S_ is generated by the [hemimetric](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Hemimetric "Hemimetric") (or [pseudo](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Pseudometric_space "Pseudometric space")-[quasimetric](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Quasimetric "Quasimetric")) d(0,1)=0![{\displaystyle d(0,1)=0}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/09c16e3d5be9d4236de09c1cfb13c34295cf5908.svg) and d(1,0)=1.![{\displaystyle d(1,0)=1.}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/fee745324442eb33aec36093f0e0a9b9394309c1.svg)

### Other properties

- There are only three [continuous maps](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Continuous_function_\(topology\) "Continuous function (topology)") from _S_ to itself: the [identity map](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Identity_function "Identity function") and the [constant maps](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Constant_map "Constant map") to 0 and 1.
- It follows that the [homeomorphism group](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Homeomorphism_group "Homeomorphism group") of _S_ is [trivial](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Trivial_group "Trivial group").

## Continuous functions to the Sierpiński space

Let _X_ be an arbitrary set. The [set of all functions](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Function_space "Function space") from _X_ to the set {0,1}![{\displaystyle \{0,1\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/28de5781698336d21c9c560fb1cbb3fb406923eb.svg) is typically denoted 2X.![{\displaystyle 2^{X}.}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/0a46e1256859c4cae839b5f3bcfb8f1f88f1542e.svg) These functions are precisely the [characteristic functions](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Indicator_function "Indicator function") of _X_. Each such function is of the form χU(x)={1x∈U0x∉Uwhere _U_ is a [subset](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Subset "Subset") of _X_. In other words, the set of functions 2X![{\displaystyle 2^{X}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/59a83de58939a4ef8e4837ab6dd554da4278f557.svg) is in [bijective](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Bijective "Bijective") correspondence with P(X),![{\displaystyle P(X),}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/d4cb09d6828a3e79dad4b330ce7a022c3851974c.svg) the [power set](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Power_set "Power set") of _X_. Every subset _U_ of _X_ has its characteristic function χU![{\displaystyle \chi _{U}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/ddf072748677ca6beb7d01a102771a72721451aa.svg) and every function from _X_ to {0,1}![{\displaystyle \{0,1\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/28de5781698336d21c9c560fb1cbb3fb406923eb.svg) is of this form.

Now suppose _X_ is a topological space and let {0,1}![{\displaystyle \{0,1\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/28de5781698336d21c9c560fb1cbb3fb406923eb.svg) have the Sierpiński topology. Then a function χU:X→S![{\displaystyle \chi _{U}:X\to S}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/b9c786d0b976de9b9225a31c98cee37266a1d8f6.svg) is [continuous](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Continuous_function_\(topology\) "Continuous function (topology)") if and only if χU−1(1)![{\displaystyle \chi _{U}^{-1}(1)}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/59160808348b241244b015a7401595cd74818ca8.svg) is open in _X_. But, by definition χU−1(1)=U.So χU![{\displaystyle \chi _{U}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/ddf072748677ca6beb7d01a102771a72721451aa.svg) is continuous if and only if _U_ is open in _X_. Let C(X,S)![{\displaystyle C(X,S)}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/e452857b2dde500c98531dd5aba0faeedfa179be.svg) denote the set of all continuous maps from _X_ to _S_ and let T(X)![{\displaystyle T(X)}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/fe67aad4eff628fcb5bb28ee6a2213d28ff12e7e.svg) denote the topology of _X_ (that is, the family of all open sets). Then we have a bijection from T(X)![{\displaystyle T(X)}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/fe67aad4eff628fcb5bb28ee6a2213d28ff12e7e.svg) to C(X,S)![{\displaystyle C(X,S)}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/e452857b2dde500c98531dd5aba0faeedfa179be.svg) which sends the open set U![{\displaystyle U}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/458a728f53b9a0274f059cd695e067c430956025.svg) to χU.![{\displaystyle \chi _{U}.}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/20b5bd29485d0ebf01164b0a85e249b5839d12c2.svg) C(X,S)≅T(X)That is, if we identify 2X![{\displaystyle 2^{X}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/59a83de58939a4ef8e4837ab6dd554da4278f557.svg) with P(X)![{\displaystyle P(X)}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/bf94167ebc67db1573e6be9e261cd0518e49e160.svg) the subset of continuous maps C(X,S)⊆2X![{\displaystyle C(X,S)\subseteq 2^{X}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/0837023a2f5fc8b86186966e15b739366efd1e72.svg) is precisely the topology of X:![{\displaystyle X:}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/380c39b7909ca067d05dafb1407c5dd2ab53e78e.svg) T(X)⊆P(X).![{\displaystyle T(X)\subseteq P(X).}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/5a0a7c60719f5ce776dd61159ed344af6b13fe41.svg)

A particularly notable example of this is the [Scott topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Scott_topology "Scott topology") for [partially ordered sets](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Partially_ordered_set "Partially ordered set"), in which the Sierpiński space becomes the [classifying space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Classifying_space "Classifying space") for open sets when the characteristic function preserves directed joins.[[5]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Sierpi%C5%84ski_space#cite_note-5)

### Categorical description

The above construction can be described nicely using the language of [category theory](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Category_theory "Category theory"). There is a [contravariant functor](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Contravariant_functor "Contravariant functor") T:Top→Set![{\displaystyle T:\mathbf {Top} \to \mathbf {Set} }](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/2f735b823a2437910b8c8ac2e1e1eba8ba7eabae.svg) from the [category of topological spaces](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Category_of_topological_spaces "Category of topological spaces") to the [category of sets](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Category_of_sets "Category of sets") which assigns each topological space X![{\displaystyle X}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/68baa052181f707c662844a465bfeeb135e82bab.svg) its set of open sets T(X)![{\displaystyle T(X)}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/fe67aad4eff628fcb5bb28ee6a2213d28ff12e7e.svg) and each continuous function f:X→Y![{\displaystyle f:X\to Y}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/abd1e080abef4bbdab67b43819c6431e7561361c.svg) the [preimage](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Preimage "Preimage") map f−1:T(Y)→T(X).The statement then becomes: the functor T![{\displaystyle T}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/ec7200acd984a1d3a3d7dc455e262fbe54f7f6e0.svg) is represented by (S,{1})![{\displaystyle (S,\{1\})}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/7b292e2dbc308747167424f6fa827f38b6f387f0.svg) where S![{\displaystyle S}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/4611d85173cd3b508e67077d4a1252c9c05abca2.svg) is the Sierpiński space. That is, T![{\displaystyle T}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/ec7200acd984a1d3a3d7dc455e262fbe54f7f6e0.svg) is [naturally isomorphic](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Naturally_isomorphic "Naturally isomorphic") to the [Hom functor](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Hom_functor "Hom functor") Hom⁡(−,S)![{\displaystyle \operatorname {Hom} (-,S)}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/1ef058dd2dec0a5289d2efbab3447f54a1bef2b8.svg) with the natural isomorphism determined by the universal element {1}∈T(S).![{\displaystyle \{1\}\in T(S).}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/ae73bf19fc6ff833c05508da377c1d5aff0c5d7b.svg) This is generalized by the notion of a [presheaf](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Presheaf "Presheaf").[[6]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Sierpi%C5%84ski_space#cite_note-6)

### The initial topology

Any topological space _X_ has the [initial topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Initial_topology "Initial topology") induced by the family C(X,S)![{\displaystyle C(X,S)}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/e452857b2dde500c98531dd5aba0faeedfa179be.svg) of continuous functions to Sierpiński space. Indeed, in order to [coarsen](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Coarser_topology "Coarser topology") the topology on _X_ one must remove open sets. But removing the open set _U_ would render χU![{\displaystyle \chi _{U}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/ddf072748677ca6beb7d01a102771a72721451aa.svg) discontinuous. So _X_ has the coarsest topology for which each function in C(X,S)![{\displaystyle C(X,S)}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/e452857b2dde500c98531dd5aba0faeedfa179be.svg) is continuous.

The family of functions C(X,S)![{\displaystyle C(X,S)}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/e452857b2dde500c98531dd5aba0faeedfa179be.svg) [separates points](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Separating_set "Separating set") in _X_ if and only if _X_ is a [T0 space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/T0_space "T0 space"). Two points x![{\displaystyle x}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/87f9e315fd7e2ba406057a97300593c4802b53e4.svg) and y![{\displaystyle y}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/b8a6208ec717213d4317e666f1ae872e00620a0d.svg) will be separated by the function χU![{\displaystyle \chi _{U}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/ddf072748677ca6beb7d01a102771a72721451aa.svg) if and only if the open set _U_ contains precisely one of the two points. This is exactly what it means for x![{\displaystyle x}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/87f9e315fd7e2ba406057a97300593c4802b53e4.svg) and y![{\displaystyle y}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/b8a6208ec717213d4317e666f1ae872e00620a0d.svg) to be [topologically distinguishable](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Topologically_distinguishable "Topologically distinguishable").

Therefore, if _X_ is T0, we can embed _X_ as a [subspace](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Subspace_\(topology\) "Subspace (topology)") of a [product](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Product_topology "Product topology") of Sierpiński spaces, where there is one copy of _S_ for each open set _U_ in _X_. The embedding map e:X→∏U∈T(X)S=ST(X)is given by e(x)U=χU(x).Since subspaces and products of T0 spaces are T0, it follows that a topological space is T0 if and only if it is [homeomorphic](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Homeomorphic "Homeomorphic") to a subspace of a power of _S_.

The [generic point](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Generic_point "Generic point") of Spec⁡(R),![{\displaystyle \operatorname {Spec} (R),}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/220b1c069f443ff84201b9e84af890514a1f016a.svg) coming from the [zero ideal](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Zero_ideal "Zero ideal"), corresponds to the open point 1, while the [special point](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Special_point "Special point") of Spec⁡(R),![{\displaystyle \operatorname {Spec} (R),}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/220b1c069f443ff84201b9e84af890514a1f016a.svg) coming from the unique [maximal ideal](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Maximal_ideal "Maximal ideal"), corresponds to the closed point 0.
