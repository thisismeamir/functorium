# Inverse of Linear Transformation

## Definition

Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over the field $\mathbb{K}$. Let $T$ be a linear transformation from $V$ into $W$. A function $\tilde{T}$ from $W$ into $V$ is said to be an inverse of $T$ if $\tilde{T}T = I_{v}$, the identity function on $V$ and $T\tilde{T} = I _{W}$, the identity function on $W$. Furthermore, $T$ is invertible if and only if $T$ is both one-one and onto. the inverse of a linear transformation $T$ is denoted by $T^{-1}$.

We can also show that such inverses are unique:

**Theorem**
Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over the field $\mathbb{K}$, and let $T:V \to W$ be an invertible linear transformation. Then $T^{-1}:W\to V$ is linear and unique.
