# Preface

There's no doubt that one of the most interesting topics in physics, which attracts a large number of students into studying the field, is the theory of Quantum Mechanics. Mysteries, counter-intuitive byproducts, and philosophical arguments that has been an ever-going dialect in science since the birth of the theory and its first amazing predictions, catches the eye of any curious mind to know more and to understand it.

But this document is not a mere discussion, or talk about what quantum mechanics is. I would try to cover a vast theme of ideas, including mathematical structures, physical systems, interpretation and philosophical debates, as one body of knowledge throughout the document. Quantum Mechanics theory is based upon simple math. Simple with respect to other theories we hold in physics (such as Classical Mechanics, General Relativity, and others), yet the results seem rather strange. This strangeness, in my opinion, doesn't come from the theory itself, but it is a product of two things.

1. We are not used to a world governed by quantum mechanics.
2. We cannot make the connection between the world we know and the world of quantum mechanics clear.

I wish I could say that by the end of this project, one would have a final, strict answer to these problems. But the truth is that these fundamental aspects, are not known, and possibly cannot be known. Despite which, I believe, that if we consider Quantum Mechanics prior to its predictions, and the problems it explained (such as Stern-Gerlach experiment), we can be more comfortable with it. 

Here we begin our journey, unexpectedly, not where everyone else does. We take ourselves first through a collection of mathematical ideas, that are the structure of our theory. Those being Linear Algebra, and Group Theory. There are many other structures that are being used in Quantum Mechanics as a living research area, even computational methods, as we encounter places that our toolkit doesn't suffice, we would extend it. 

Using these mathematical ideas, we start by constructing the theory from ground up. Quantum Theory is not mere math, it is a physical theory that has been formulated using mathematics, therefore, principles, interpretations, and labeling stuff is necessary to be able to talk about reality.

After our foundations is solid enough to talk about natural systems, we'd go on by examining them. We start by simple systems (which are mostly hypothetical, simplified models of real world phenomena), gather more knowledge about them and then go on to more complex ones. Systems that cannot be solved exactly, and systems that bring out rather odd artifacts of the theory itself.

Considerations of systems can give us the experience necessary to look at history more cleanly, we then start by looking at paradoxes, problems, and ad-hocs that shaped the theory in the early 20th century. Perhaps when reading this chapter one might say *"It all looks too simple"* but keep in mind that you're encountering them after you've been taught about the standard formulation of theory. 

Quantum Mechanics is not about a specific system. It is a framework. And frameworks can be applied in many regions of natural studies. Therefore, our discussion would continue to talk about the applications, and usage of Quantum Mechanics as a framework. As an example, from the trends of news and research papers, it is obvious that quantum information science is going to play a dominant role in the real of quantum mechanics as a framework. We would discuss theories of quantum statistical mechanics, quantum fields, information and computation, and more advanced topics as we move along.

Therefore, this project is far from being a solid book of my understanding. I prefer to extend it as much as possible, with the idea being to stay solid, and uniform throughout the chapters and parts. It may be updated each year with new topics, understanding, researches, and models. Let us begin the journey as soon as possible, so that maybe a time would arrive that we understand quantum mechanics in all it's simplicity, and complications.
