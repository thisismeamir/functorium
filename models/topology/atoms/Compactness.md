In [mathematics](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Mathematics "Mathematics"), specifically [general topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/General_topology "General topology"), **compactness** is a property that seeks to generalize the notion of a [closed](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Closed_set "Closed set") and [bounded](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Bounded_set "Bounded set") subset of [Euclidean space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Euclidean_space "Euclidean space").

The idea is that every infinite sequence of points has [_limiting values_](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Limit_\(mathematics\) "Limit (mathematics)"). For example, the [real line](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Real_line "Real line") is not compact since the sequence of [natural numbers](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Natural_numbers "Natural numbers") has no real limiting value. The open [interval](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Interval_\(mathematics\) "Interval (mathematics)") (0,1) is not compact because it excludes the limiting values 0 and 1, whereas the closed interval [0,1] is compact. Similarly, the space of [rational numbers](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Rational_number "Rational number") $\mathbb {Q}$  is not compact, because every [irrational number](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Irrational_number "Irrational number") is the limit of the rational numbers that are lower than it. 

On the other hand, the [_extended_ real number line](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Extended_real_numbers "Extended real numbers") is compact, since it contains both infinities. There are many ways to make this heuristic notion precise. These ways usually agree in a [metric space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Metric_space "Metric space"), but may not be [equivalent](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Logical_equivalence "Logical equivalence") in other [topological spaces](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Topological_space "Topological space").

---

Various definitions of compactness may apply, depending on the level of generality. A subset of [Euclidean space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Euclidean_space "Euclidean space") in particular is compact if and only if it is [closed](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Closed_set "Closed set") and [bounded](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Bounded_set "Bounded set"). This implies, by the [Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Bolzano%E2%80%93Weierstrass_theorem "Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem"), that any infinite [sequence](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Sequence_\(mathematics\) "Sequence (mathematics)") from the set has a [subsequence](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Subsequence "Subsequence") that converges to a point in the set. Various equivalent notions of compactness, such as [sequential compactness](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Sequential_compactness "Sequential compactness") and [limit point compactness](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Limit_point_compact "Limit point compact"), can be developed in general [metric spaces](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Metric_space "Metric space").[[3]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-:0-3)

In contrast, the different notions of compactness are not equivalent in general [topological spaces](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Topological_space "Topological space"), and the most useful notion of compactness—originally called _bicompactness_—is defined using [covers](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Cover_\(topology\) "Cover (topology)") consisting of [open sets](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Open_set "Open set") (see _Open cover definition_ below). That this form of compactness holds for closed and bounded subsets of Euclidean space is known as the [Heine–Borel theorem](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Heine%E2%80%93Borel_theorem "Heine–Borel theorem"). Compactness, when defined in this manner, often allows one to take information that is known [locally](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Local_property "Local property")—in a [neighbourhood](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Neighbourhood_\(mathematics\) "Neighbourhood (mathematics)") of each point of the space—and to extend it to information that holds globally throughout the space. An example of this phenomenon is Dirichlet's theorem, to which it was originally applied by Heine, that a continuous function on a compact interval is [uniformly continuous](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Uniformly_continuous "Uniformly continuous"); here, continuity is a local property of the function, and uniform continuity the corresponding global property.

### Open cover definition

Formally, a [topological space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Topological_space "Topological space") X is called _compact_ if every [open cover](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Open_cover "Open cover") of X has a [finite](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Finite_set "Finite set") [subcover](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Subcover "Subcover").[[7]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-7) That is, X is compact if for every collection C of open subsets[[8]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-8) of X such that

X=⋃S∈CS ,

there is a **finite** subcollection F ⊆ C such that

X=⋃S∈FS .

Some branches of mathematics such as [algebraic geometry](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Algebraic_geometry "Algebraic geometry"), typically influenced by the French school of [Bourbaki](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Nicolas_Bourbaki "Nicolas Bourbaki"), use the term _quasi-compact_ for the general notion, and reserve the term _compact_ for topological spaces that are both [Hausdorff](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Hausdorff_space "Hausdorff space") and _quasi-compact_. A compact set is sometimes referred to as a _compactum_, plural _compacta_.

### Compactness of subsets

A subset K of a topological space X is compact if for every arbitrary collection C of open subsets of X such that

K⊆⋃S∈CS ,

there is a finite subcollection F ⊆ C such that

K⊆⋃S∈FS .

Equivalently, K is compact as a subset of X if and only if the topological space K is compact in the [subspace topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Subspace_topology "Subspace topology"). In particular, if K⊂Y⊂X![{\displaystyle K\subset Y\subset X}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/757ee9eb2ab79a87b727976b50f1ccfd14a221a4.svg), with subset Y equipped with the subspace topology, then K is compact in Y if and only if K is compact in X. Furthermore, the compactness of K as a subset of a topological space X is independent of the embedding, provided that the subspace topology on K is the same.

### Characterization

If X is a topological space then the following are equivalent:

1. X is compact; i.e., every [open cover](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Open_cover "Open cover") of X has a finite [subcover](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Subcover "Subcover").
2. X has a sub-base such that every cover of the space, by members of the sub-base, has a finite subcover ([Alexander's sub-base theorem](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Alexander's_sub-base_theorem "Alexander's sub-base theorem")).
3. X is [Lindelöf](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Lindel%C3%B6f_space "Lindelöf space") and [countably compact](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Countably_compact "Countably compact").[[9]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHowes1995xxvi%E2%80%93xxviii-9)
4. Any collection of closed subsets of X with the [finite intersection property](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Finite_intersection_property "Finite intersection property") has nonempty intersection.
5. Every [net](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Net_\(mathematics\) "Net (mathematics)") on X has a convergent subnet (see the article on [nets](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Net_\(mathematics\) "Net (mathematics)") for a proof).
6. Every [filter](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Filters_in_topology "Filters in topology") on X has a convergent refinement.
7. Every net on X has a cluster point.
8. Every filter on X has a cluster point.
9. Every [ultrafilter](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Ultrafilter_\(set_theory\) "Ultrafilter (set theory)") on X converges to at least one point.
10. Every infinite subset of X has a complete accumulation point.[[10]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-10)
11. For every topological space Y, the projection X×Y→Y![{\displaystyle X\times Y\to Y}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/66e9d4154e92a7e0f561725fe46b479499743d82.svg) is a [closed mapping](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Closed_mapping "Closed mapping")[[11]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-Bourbaki-11) (see [proper map](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Proper_map "Proper map")).
12. Every open cover linearly ordered by subset inclusion contains X.[[12]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMack1967-12)

Bourbaki defines a compact space (quasi-compact space) as a topological space where each filter has a cluster point (i.e., 8. in the above).[[13]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-BourbakiDefinition-13)

#### Euclidean space

For any [subset](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Subset "Subset") A of [Euclidean space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Euclidean_space "Euclidean space"), A is compact if and only if it is [closed](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Closed_set "Closed set") and [bounded](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Bounded_set "Bounded set"); this is the [Heine–Borel theorem](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Heine%E2%80%93Borel_theorem "Heine–Borel theorem").

As a [Euclidean space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Euclidean_space "Euclidean space") is a metric space, the conditions in the next subsection also apply to all of its subsets. Of all of the equivalent conditions, it is in practice easiest to verify that a subset is closed and bounded, for example, for a closed [interval](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Interval_\(mathematics\) "Interval (mathematics)") or closed n-ball.

#### Metric spaces

For any metric space (_X_, _d_), the following are equivalent (assuming [countable choice](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Countable_choice "Countable choice")):

1. (_X_, _d_) is compact.
2. (_X_, _d_) is [complete](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Completeness_\(topology\) "Completeness (topology)") and [totally bounded](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Totally_bounded "Totally bounded") (this is also equivalent to compactness for [uniform spaces](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Uniform_space "Uniform space")).[[14]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-14)
3. (_X_, _d_) is sequentially compact; that is, every [sequence](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Sequence "Sequence") in X has a convergent subsequence whose limit is in X (this is also equivalent to compactness for [first-countable](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/First-countable "First-countable") [uniform spaces](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Uniform_space "Uniform space")).
4. (_X_, _d_) is [limit point compact](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Limit_point_compact "Limit point compact") (also called weakly countably compact); that is, every infinite subset of X has at least one limit point in X.
5. (_X_, _d_) is [countably compact](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Countably_compact "Countably compact"); that is, every countable open cover of X has a finite subcover.
6. X is empty or (_X_, _d_) is an image of a continuous function from the [Cantor set](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Cantor_set "Cantor set").[[15]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-15)
7. Every decreasing nested sequence of nonempty closed subsets _S_1 ⊇ _S_2 ⊇ ... in (_X_, _d_) has a nonempty intersection.
8. Every increasing nested sequence of proper open subsets _S_1 ⊆ _S_2 ⊆ ... in (_X_, _d_) fails to cover X.

A compact metric space (_X_, _d_) also satisfies the following properties:

1. [Lebesgue's number lemma](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Lebesgue's_number_lemma "Lebesgue's number lemma"): For every open cover of X, there exists a number _δ_ > 0 such that every subset of X of diameter < δ is contained in some member of the cover.
2. (_X_, _d_) is [second-countable](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Second-countable_space "Second-countable space"), [separable](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Separable_space "Separable space") and [Lindelöf](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Lindel%C3%B6f_space "Lindelöf space") – these three conditions are equivalent for metric spaces. The converse is not true; e.g., a countable discrete space satisfies these three conditions, but is not compact.
3. X is closed and bounded (as a subset of any metric space whose restricted metric is d). The converse may fail for a non-Euclidean space; e.g. the [real line](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Real_line "Real line") equipped with the [discrete metric](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Discrete_metric "Discrete metric") is closed and bounded but not compact, as the collection of all [singletons](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Singleton_\(mathematics\) "Singleton (mathematics)") of the space is an open cover which admits no finite subcover. It is complete but not totally bounded.

#### Ordered spaces

For an ordered space (_X_, <) (i.e. a totally ordered set equipped with the order topology), the following are equivalent:

1. (_X_, <) is compact.
2. Every subset of X has a supremum (i.e. a least upper bound) in X.
3. Every subset of X has an infimum (i.e. a greatest lower bound) in X.
4. Every nonempty closed subset of X has a maximum and a minimum element.

An ordered space satisfying (any one of) these conditions is called a complete lattice.

In addition, the following are equivalent for all ordered spaces (_X_, <), and (assuming [countable choice](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Countable_choice "Countable choice")) are true whenever (_X_, <) is compact (the converse in general fails if (_X_, <) is not also metrizable):

1. Every sequence in (_X_, <) has a subsequence that converges in (_X_, <).
2. Every monotone increasing sequence in X converges to a unique limit in X.
3. Every monotone decreasing sequence in X converges to a unique limit in X.
4. Every decreasing nested sequence of nonempty closed subsets S1 ⊇ S2 ⊇ ... in (_X_, <) has a nonempty intersection.
5. Every increasing nested sequence of proper open subsets S1 ⊆ S2 ⊆ ... in (_X_, <) fails to cover X.

#### Characterization by continuous functions

Let X be a topological space and C(_X_) the ring of real continuous functions on X. For each _p_ ∈ _X_, the evaluation map evp:C(X)→R![{\displaystyle \operatorname {ev} _{p}\colon C(X)\to \mathbb {R} }](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/7e8866d64e224786e38571ce6adabbc5fd3923bc.svg) given by ev_p_(_f_) = _f_(_p_) is a ring homomorphism. The [kernel](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Kernel_\(algebra\) "Kernel (algebra)") of ev_p_ is a [maximal ideal](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Maximal_ideal "Maximal ideal"), since the [residue field](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Residue_field "Residue field") C(_X_)/ker ev_p_ is the field of real numbers, by the [first isomorphism theorem](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/First_isomorphism_theorem "First isomorphism theorem"). A topological space X is [pseudocompact](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Pseudocompact_space "Pseudocompact space") if and only if every maximal ideal in C(_X_) has residue field the real numbers. For [completely regular spaces](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Completely_regular_space "Completely regular space"), this is equivalent to every maximal ideal being the kernel of an evaluation homomorphism.[[16]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-16) There are pseudocompact spaces that are not compact, though.

In general, for non-pseudocompact spaces there are always maximal ideals m in C(_X_) such that the residue field C(_X_)/_m_ is a ([non-Archimedean](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Non-archimedean_field "Non-archimedean field")) [hyperreal field](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Hyperreal_field "Hyperreal field"). The framework of [non-standard analysis](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Non-standard_analysis "Non-standard analysis") allows for the following alternative characterization of compactness:[[17]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-17) a topological space X is compact if and only if every point x of the natural extension _*X_ is [infinitely close](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Infinitesimal "Infinitesimal") to a point _x_0 of X (more precisely, x is contained in the [monad](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Monad_\(non-standard_analysis\) "Monad (non-standard analysis)") of _x_0).

#### Hyperreal definition

A space X is compact if its [hyperreal extension](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Hyperreal_number "Hyperreal number") _*X_ (constructed, for example, by the [ultrapower construction](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Ultrapower_construction "Ultrapower construction")) has the property that every point of _*X_ is infinitely close to some point of _X_ ⊂ _*X_. For example, an open real interval _X_ = (0, 1) is not compact because its hyperreal extension *(0,1) contains infinitesimals, which are infinitely close to 0, which is not a point of X.

## Sufficient conditions

- A closed subset of a compact space is compact.[[18]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-18)
- A finite [union](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Union_\(set_theory\) "Union (set theory)") of compact sets is compact.
- A [continuous](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Continuous_function_\(topology\) "Continuous function (topology)") image of a compact space is compact.[[19]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-19)
- The intersection of any non-empty collection of compact subsets of a Hausdorff space is compact (and closed).
    - If X is not Hausdorff then the intersection of two compact subsets may fail to be compact.[[a]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-20)
- The [product](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Product_topology "Product topology") of any collection of compact spaces is compact. (This is [Tychonoff's theorem](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Tychonoff's_theorem "Tychonoff's theorem"), which is equivalent to the [axiom of choice](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Axiom_of_choice "Axiom of choice").)
- In a [metrizable space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Metrizable_space "Metrizable space"), a subset is compact if and only if it is [sequentially compact](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Sequentially_compact "Sequentially compact") (assuming [countable choice](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Axiom_of_countable_choice "Axiom of countable choice")).
- A finite set endowed with any topology is compact.

## Properties of compact spaces

- A space in which every compact subset is closed is called a [KC space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/KC_space "KC space").
    - A compact subset of a [Hausdorff space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Hausdorff_space "Hausdorff space") X is closed.
    - If X is not Hausdorff then a compact subset of X may fail to be a closed subset of X.[[b]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-21)[[c]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-22)
    - If X is not Hausdorff then the closure of a compact set may fail to be compact.[[d]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-23)
    - If X is not Hausdorff then it can still be the case that every compact subset is closed.[[e]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-24)
- In any [topological vector space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Topological_vector_space "Topological vector space") (TVS), a compact subset is [complete](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Complete_space "Complete space"). However, every non-Hausdorff TVS contains compact (and thus complete) subsets that are _not_ closed.
- If A and B are disjoint compact subsets of a Hausdorff space X, then there exist disjoint open sets U and V in X such that _A_ ⊆ _U_ and _B_ ⊆ _V_.
- A continuous bijection from a compact space into a Hausdorff space is a [homeomorphism](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Homeomorphism "Homeomorphism").
- A compact Hausdorff space is [normal](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Normal_space "Normal space") and [regular](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Regular_space "Regular space").
- If a space X is compact and Hausdorff, then no finer topology on X is compact and no coarser topology on X is Hausdorff.
- If a subset of a metric space (_X_, _d_) is compact then it is d-bounded.

### Functions and compact spaces

Since a [continuous](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Continuous_function_\(topology\) "Continuous function (topology)") image of a compact space is compact, the [extreme value theorem](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Extreme_value_theorem "Extreme value theorem") holds for such spaces: a continuous real-valued function on a nonempty compact space is bounded above and attains its supremum.[[20]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-25) (Slightly more generally, this is true for an upper semicontinuous function.) As a sort of converse to the above statements, the pre-image of a compact space under a [proper map](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Proper_map "Proper map") is compact.

### Compactifications

Main article: [Compactification (mathematics)](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compactification_\(mathematics\) "Compactification (mathematics)")

Every topological space X is an open [dense subspace](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Dense_topological_subspace "Dense topological subspace") of a compact space having at most one point more than X, by the [Alexandroff one-point compactification](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Alexandroff_one-point_compactification "Alexandroff one-point compactification"). By the same construction, every [locally compact](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Locally_compact "Locally compact") Hausdorff space X is an open dense subspace of a compact Hausdorff space having at most one point more than X.

### Ordered compact spaces

A nonempty compact subset of the [real numbers](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Real_number "Real number") has a greatest element and a least element.

Let X be a [simply ordered](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Total_order "Total order") set endowed with the [order topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Order_topology "Order topology"). Then X is compact if and only if X is a [complete lattice](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Complete_lattice "Complete lattice") (i.e. all subsets have suprema and infima).[[21]](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Compact_space#cite_note-26)

## Examples

- Any [finite topological space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Finite_topological_space "Finite topological space"), including the [empty set](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Empty_set "Empty set"), is compact. More generally, any space with a [finite topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Finite_topology "Finite topology") (only finitely many open sets) is compact; this includes in particular the [trivial topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Trivial_topology "Trivial topology").
- Any space carrying the [cofinite topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Cofinite_topology "Cofinite topology") is compact.
- Any [locally compact](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Locally_compact "Locally compact") Hausdorff space can be turned into a compact space by adding a single point to it, by means of [Alexandroff one-point compactification](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Alexandroff_one-point_compactification "Alexandroff one-point compactification"). The one-point compactification of R![{\displaystyle \mathbb {R} }](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/786849c765da7a84dbc3cce43e96aad58a5868dc.svg) is homeomorphic to the circle **S**1; the one-point compactification of R2![{\displaystyle \mathbb {R} ^{2}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/e150115ab9f63023215109595b76686a1ff890fd.svg) is homeomorphic to the sphere **S**2. Using the one-point compactification, one can also easily construct compact spaces which are not Hausdorff, by starting with a non-Hausdorff space.
- The [right order topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Right_order_topology "Right order topology") or [left order topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Left_order_topology "Left order topology") on any bounded [totally ordered set](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Totally_ordered_set "Totally ordered set") is compact. In particular, [Sierpiński space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Sierpi%C5%84ski_space "Sierpiński space") is compact.
- No [discrete space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Discrete_space "Discrete space") with an infinite number of points is compact. The collection of all [singletons](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Singleton_\(mathematics\) "Singleton (mathematics)") of the space is an open cover which admits no finite subcover. Finite discrete spaces are compact.
- In R![{\displaystyle \mathbb {R} }](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/786849c765da7a84dbc3cce43e96aad58a5868dc.svg) carrying the [lower limit topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Lower_limit_topology "Lower limit topology"), no uncountable set is compact.
- In the [cocountable topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Cocountable_topology "Cocountable topology") on an uncountable set, no infinite set is compact. Like the previous example, the space as a whole is not [locally compact](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Locally_compact "Locally compact") but is still [Lindelöf](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Lindel%C3%B6f_space "Lindelöf space").
- The closed [unit interval](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Unit_interval "Unit interval") [0, 1] is compact. This follows from the [Heine–Borel theorem](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Heine%E2%80%93Borel_theorem "Heine–Borel theorem"). The open interval (0, 1) is not compact: the [open cover](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Open_cover "Open cover") (1n,1−1n)![{\textstyle \left({\frac {1}{n}},1-{\frac {1}{n}}\right)}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/c2b7b7ae8290bf973c0d47bfc9e99105a9e194ea.svg) for n = 3, 4, ...  does not have a finite subcover. Similarly, the set of _[rational numbers](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Rational_numbers "Rational numbers")_ in the closed interval [0,1] is not compact: the sets of rational numbers in the intervals [0,1π−1n] and [1π+1n,1]![{\textstyle \left[0,{\frac {1}{\pi }}-{\frac {1}{n}}\right]{\text{ and }}\left[{\frac {1}{\pi }}+{\frac {1}{n}},1\right]}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/cd15c0d260a029851c6acad5e36712fbb0f88c71.svg) cover all the rationals in [0, 1] for n = 4, 5, ...  but this cover does not have a finite subcover. Here, the sets are open in the subspace topology even though they are not open as subsets of R![{\displaystyle \mathbb {R} }](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/786849c765da7a84dbc3cce43e96aad58a5868dc.svg).
- The set R![{\displaystyle \mathbb {R} }](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/786849c765da7a84dbc3cce43e96aad58a5868dc.svg) of all real numbers is not compact as there is a cover of open intervals that does not have a finite subcover. For example, intervals (n − 1, n + 1) , where n takes all integer values in **Z**, cover R![{\displaystyle \mathbb {R} }](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/786849c765da7a84dbc3cce43e96aad58a5868dc.svg) but there is no finite subcover.
- On the other hand, the [extended real number line](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Extended_real_number_line "Extended real number line") carrying the analogous topology _is_ compact; note that the cover described above would never reach the points at infinity and thus would _not_ cover the extended real line. In fact, the set has the [homeomorphism](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Homeomorphism "Homeomorphism") to [−1, 1] of mapping each infinity to its corresponding unit and every real number to its sign multiplied by the unique number in the positive part of interval that results in its absolute value when divided by one minus itself, and since homeomorphisms preserve covers, the Heine-Borel property can be inferred.
- For every [natural number](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Natural_number "Natural number") n, the [n-sphere](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/N-sphere "N-sphere") is compact. Again from the Heine–Borel theorem, the closed unit ball of any finite-dimensional [normed vector space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Normed_vector_space "Normed vector space") is compact. This is not true for infinite dimensions; in fact, a normed vector space is finite-dimensional if and only if its [closed unit ball](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Closed_unit_ball "Closed unit ball") is compact.
- On the other hand, the closed unit ball of the dual of a normed space is compact for the weak-* topology. ([Alaoglu's theorem](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Alaoglu's_theorem "Alaoglu's theorem"))
- The [Cantor set](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Cantor_set "Cantor set") is compact. In fact, every non-empty compact metric space is a continuous image of the Cantor set.
- Consider the set K of all functions _f_ : **R** → [0, 1] from the real number line to the closed unit interval, and define a topology on K so that a sequence {fn}![{\displaystyle \{f_{n}\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/009619e33115347a277b099ff493347bdd5776aa.svg) in K converges towards _f_ ∈ _K_ if and only if {fn(x)}![{\displaystyle \{f_{n}(x)\}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/db082772469c125b07c34485abb261915262e57f.svg) converges towards _f_(_x_) for all real numbers x. There is only one such topology; it is called the topology of [pointwise convergence](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Pointwise_convergence "Pointwise convergence") or the [product topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Product_topology "Product topology"). Then K is a compact topological space; this follows from the [Tychonoff theorem](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Tychonoff_theorem "Tychonoff theorem").
- A subset of the Banach space of real-valued continuous functions on a compact Hausdorff space is relatively compact if and only if it is equicontinuous and pointwise bounded ([Arzelà–Ascoli theorem](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Arzel%C3%A0%E2%80%93Ascoli_theorem "Arzelà–Ascoli theorem")).
- Consider the set K of all functions _f_ : [0, 1] → [0, 1] satisfying the [Lipschitz condition](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Lipschitz_condition "Lipschitz condition") |_f_(_x_) − _f_(_y_)| ≤ |_x_ − _y_| for all _x_, _y_ ∈ [0,1]. Consider on K the metric induced by the [uniform distance](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Uniform_convergence "Uniform convergence") d(f,g)=supx∈[0,1]|f(x)−g(x)|.![{\displaystyle d(f,g)=\sup _{x\in [0,1]}|f(x)-g(x)|.}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/d4968cb9f2028481e49bb5c4a09a337ae1840ce7.svg) Then by the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem the space K is compact.
- The [spectrum](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Spectrum_of_an_operator "Spectrum of an operator") of any [bounded linear operator](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Bounded_linear_operator "Bounded linear operator") on a [Banach space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Banach_space "Banach space") is a nonempty compact subset of the [complex numbers](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Complex_number "Complex number") C![{\displaystyle \mathbb {C} }](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/f9add4085095b9b6d28d045fd9c92c2c09f549a7.svg). Conversely, any compact subset of C![{\displaystyle \mathbb {C} }](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/f9add4085095b9b6d28d045fd9c92c2c09f549a7.svg) arises in this manner, as the spectrum of some bounded linear operator. For instance, a diagonal operator on the Hilbert space [ℓ2![{\displaystyle \ell ^{2}}](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/91f1f909abd70bd3d8fff0f7ae1ac23052387e18.svg)](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Sequence_spaces#%E2%84%93p_spaces "Sequence spaces") may have any compact nonempty subset of C![{\displaystyle \mathbb {C} }](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/_assets_/eb734a37dd21ce173a46342d1cc64c92/f9add4085095b9b6d28d045fd9c92c2c09f549a7.svg) as spectrum.
- The space of Borel [probability measures](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Probability_measure "Probability measure") on a compact Hausdorff space is compact for the [vague topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Vague_topology "Vague topology"), by the Alaoglu theorem.
- A collection of probability measures on the Borel sets of Euclidean space is called _tight_ if, for any positive epsilon, there exists a compact subset containing all but at most epsilon of the mass of each of the measures. Helly's theorem then asserts that a collection of probability measures is relatively compact for the vague topology if and only if it is tight.

### Algebraic examples

- [Topological groups](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Topological_group "Topological group") such as an [orthogonal group](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Orthogonal_group "Orthogonal group") are compact, while groups such as a [general linear group](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/General_linear_group "General linear group") are not.
- Since the [p-adic integers](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/P-adic_numbers "P-adic numbers") are [homeomorphic](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Homeomorphic "Homeomorphic") to the Cantor set, they form a compact set.
- Any [global field](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Global_field "Global field") _K_ is a discrete additive subgroup of its [adele ring](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Adele_ring "Adele ring"), and the quotient space is compact. This was used in [John Tate](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/John_Tate_\(mathematician\) "John Tate (mathematician)")'s [thesis](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Tate's_thesis "Tate's thesis") to allow [harmonic analysis](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Harmonic_analysis "Harmonic analysis") to be used in [number theory](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Number_theory "Number theory").
- The [spectrum](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Spectrum_of_a_ring "Spectrum of a ring") of any [commutative ring](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Commutative_ring "Commutative ring") with the [Zariski topology](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Zariski_topology "Zariski topology") (that is, the set of all prime ideals) is compact, but never [Hausdorff](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Hausdorff_space "Hausdorff space") (except in trivial cases). In algebraic geometry, such topological spaces are examples of quasi-compact [schemes](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Scheme_\(mathematics\) "Scheme (mathematics)"), "quasi" referring to the non-Hausdorff nature of the topology.
- The spectrum of a [Boolean algebra](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Boolean_algebra "Boolean algebra") is compact, a fact which is part of the [Stone representation theorem](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Stone_representation_theorem "Stone representation theorem"). [Stone spaces](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Stone_space "Stone space"), compact [totally disconnected](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Totally_disconnected_space "Totally disconnected space") Hausdorff spaces, form the abstract framework in which these spectra are studied. Such spaces are also useful in the study of [profinite groups](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Profinite_group "Profinite group").
- The [structure space](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Structure_space "Structure space") of a commutative unital [Banach algebra](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Banach_algebra "Banach algebra") is a compact Hausdorff space.
- The [Hilbert cube](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Hilbert_cube "Hilbert cube") is compact, again a consequence of Tychonoff's theorem.
- A [profinite group](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Profinite_group "Profinite group") (e.g. [Galois group](zim://6112eb33-9137-7530-79f2-04ea02fbb402.zim/Galois_group "Galois group")) is compact.
